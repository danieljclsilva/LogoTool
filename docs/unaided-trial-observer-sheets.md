## Observer method

Read each task outcome in ordinary language. Let the participant choose their
route. Record their first action, pauses, misunderstandings, exact words and any
help. If assistance becomes necessary, record its time and wording, then let the
participant continue. Distinguish independent success, assisted success and
incomplete tasks. Record elapsed time for each task.

Use a fresh participant for each browser where possible. Record prior exposure
when a participant repeats the flow. Synthetic practice assets can establish
workflow usability. A real measured product supplies physical acceptance evidence.

## Solo rehearsal while people or hardware are unavailable

Use an author-led rehearsal now and keep the independent trial rows pending.
Record prior familiarity and the actual OS/browser. A rehearsal on the available
computer supplies development evidence. Record Windows operator acceptance
when a representative Windows machine becomes available.

Use the generated `dist/dev/solo-walkthrough.zip` or its extracted folder.
The kit contains the current staff HTML, a self-contained customer HTML,
two synthetic models and a PNG mark. Open the HTML files directly after unzip
with networking disabled. The models and 300 mm task span are synthetic
workflow stimuli. Physical acceptance uses a measured real product later.

1. Spend up to 20 minutes in `Logo Station.html`. Start a job, add `Product A.glb`
   and `Mark A.png`, pick a reference span and enter the 300 mm task value,
   define an area, place a logo, save and reopen. If time permits, create the
   customer approval files and retain the office copy. Note every point where
   you must guess or consult instructions.
2. Spend up to 15 minutes in your issued customer file, or use the supplied
   `Customer.html` when that file is still pending. Record which file you use. Set linked width and height,
   explicitly unlock for independent dimensions, undo, save/reopen, review and
   confirm. The supplied file lets the customer walkthrough continue when the
   staff path is blocked.
3. Use remaining time for the excluded-product and 22,000 mm checks on separate
   staff copies, and customer replacement/close warnings. Record saved output
   dimensions and exact warning text. If you issued your own customer file,
   open its return with the office copy and inspect the measurements. Stop at
   the session cap.
4. Record first actions, help consulted, ease ratings and any blockers in the
   same sheets. A solo rehearsal contributes one author-led record. The proposed
   independent participant completion threshold remains pending.

If an unfamiliar colleague or friend becomes available, they can try the
customer task with consent. Record their actual role and prior exposure.
Keep completed sheets, recordings and customer-specific files privately.

## Trial timebox

Keep the whole session within 60 minutes, including welcome, consent,
core tasks and debrief. Allow 5 minutes for consent and setup, 40 minutes for
core tasks, 10 minutes for debrief and 5 minutes of contingency. Run the core
chain first. Use a separate follow-up session for the remaining tasks.
If the cap approaches, stop the current task cleanly, record remaining
tasks as not attempted, and move them to a follow-up session.

## Post-task ease rating

After each attempted task, ask “How easy or difficult was that task?”
Record a 1–7 rating in the Ease column
(1 very difficult, 7 very easy). Do not lead the participant.
Record "not attempted" for tasks not reached within the timebox.

## Incident severity and stopping rule

Record severity for every incident.

- Critical: data loss or wrong physical measurements in the saved/returned result.
- Blocker: a core task or recovery of its valid output cannot be completed through the available controls.
- Recoverable friction: participant struggles but completes the task.
- Cosmetic: wording, layout or minor visual issue only.

Stop the trial after any critical incident, or after two participants
hit the same core blocker. Fix and recheck before continuing with
further participants.

The scripted 22,000 mm entry is the calibration task stimulus. Record whether
the warning is noticed and the value corrected. Retaining that value in the
saved result is a critical incident.

## Proposed formative pass rule

Use the following proposed formative criterion and record its adoption before
the sessions. The initial pilot supplies observations for refining the trial.

- Zero critical incidents.
- Zero unresolved core blockers.
- At least 4 of 5 participants per role complete the full core chain unaided.

The initial 2 staff + 2 customer sessions are a pilot. Fix shared
blockers found in the pilot, then expand to 5 participants per role
for the full formative round.

## Core chain and priority

For staff, the core chain is start job, calibrate, define area, place two logos,
save/reopen, issue approval with the office copy, then inspect the customer return.
For customers it is open, place, set linked width and height, review/confirm/save,
then reopen and verify. Every step must succeed unaided for a core-chain success.
Record tasks that time out as incomplete for this criterion.

After the core chain, prioritize the excluded-product and mistyped-calibration
staff checks, and the customer replacement guard and unsaved-close checks.
Navigation, Undo, independent sizing and adding/removing logos can use the
follow-up session. Retain task-level results across sessions and report the
build SHA for each. Assess the full chain on one build after a blocking fix.

over, what the size and shape controls meant, and where they were uncertain.
Record their words and the task that prompted each observation.
